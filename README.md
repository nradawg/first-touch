**Use this when** you are writing the first cold email, DM, or connection note
to someone who has never heard of you.

## What I do

I list everything you could say to this person. Then I strike every line I
cannot prove. Whatever survives is the message. If nothing survives, I tell you
that and hand you the one lookup that would give you something real to say.

That is the entire method, and it is the opposite of the usual one, which is to
write a confident message and backfill the reason.

## The strike list

Every candidate line gets a label, and only two labels get to stay:

| label | fate |
|---|---|
| `CHECKED` | can stay, and the message can lean on it |
| `FROM YOU` | can stay if you can point at where you learned it |
| `GUESSED` | struck. It does not appear in the message in any form |

A `GUESSED` line cannot be softened into the message with "I imagine" or
"it seems like." Hedged invention reads worse than silence, because they know
you do not know and now they know you are guessing out loud.

## What makes a line provable

Things you can check without an account: their own site, their own postings,
their own public filings, a page that loads. Things you cannot: how they feel
about their current tool, whether they are frustrated, what their priorities
are this quarter, whether they are growing. Those are inferences, and an
inference stated as an observation is the tell.

Then three constraints on what is left:

- **One provable thing, not three.** Three reads as research theater.
- **Ask for a reply, not a meeting.** A question beats a calendar link in a
  first message because it is cheaper to answer.
- **Write into the field.** Connection notes and DMs cap length and the box
  counts down as you type. Write to the box, not to a limit you remember, and
  they change.

```
TO: Dana Whitaker, Operations Manager, Harbor Line Freight

  They run no dispatch software                     GUESSED. STRUCK.
  She is frustrated with manual scheduling          GUESSED. STRUCK.

  You helped a similar carrier                      FROM YOU, but it is
                                                    about you. CUT, not
                                                    struck, just not first.

SURVIVES: the two open dispatcher roles.

THE MESSAGE
  "Dana, you have two dispatcher seats open. Curious whether that is
   volume or turnover, since the answer changes what I would even say
   next. Either way, good luck filling them."

WHY THIS SHAPE
  One fact, one question, no link, no pitch, no claim about her business.
  The question is answerable in four words, which is the point.

OPEN QUESTION
  Is she the one who feels the dispatch pain, or is that a supervisor
  under her? Lookup: their team page, or just ask her in reply two.

6 candidates. 3 struck, 1 checked fact carries the message.
```

## Limits

I can only check what is public and loads. Behind a login, inside their CRM, or
in anyone's head is unavailable to me, so some people will have nothing provable
about them and the honest output is "nothing to say yet, go look here." I also
cannot tell you whether this lands. One message is one sample.

The trap: the invented observation. A naive version writes "I noticed you are
scaling the ops team" because it is probably true and it sounds warm. It is the
single most recognizable pattern in cold outreach, it is indistinguishable from
a template with a merge field, and when it is wrong it is unrecoverable. Say
one true small thing instead.

## How this one knows what it knows

Every answer it gives itself carries a label: `CHECKED` (it verified it,
and says how), `FROM YOU` (you said it, and it quotes where), or
`GUESSED` (it could not verify). Then it counts the guesses, so you
correct what is wrong instead of trusting confident output built on
silent assumptions.

> Never ask for something you can check.
> Never assume something you cannot check.
